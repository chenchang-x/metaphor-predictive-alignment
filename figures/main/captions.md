# Figure Legends

**Fig. 1 | Inputs and item-level scores for the two tasks.** Qwen3.5-9B performs continuation generation and explicit judgement. **a**, A retained example pairs the original expression caught (M) with the apt substitute took (A) and the inapt substitute grabbed (I). Prefixes end after three shared post-target words. Each condition provides 32 five-token continuations per seed across three seeds. Set distance is the mean pairwise cosine distance (a difference in representation direction) between separately encoded five-token continuations, standardized using a shared reference; see Methods for computation details. Positive Δᵢ = dᵢ(M,I) − dᵢ(M,A) indicates closer alignment of M continuations with A. **b**, Explicit judgements use either the same target-marked contextual prefix or the target word alone, with matched candidates, questions and response forms. Apt-minus-inapt log response probabilities are balanced across candidate orders to obtain S_context and S_word; C is their difference. The two RQ2 models combine these scores with continuation alignment for the same 595 target items. The figure shows real inputs, with no generated continuation examples. Source data are provided.

**Fig. 2 | Distribution of item-level continuation alignment.** The histogram contains scores averaged across the three generation seeds for all 595 target items, using equal-width bins of 0.01 with zero as a bin edge. Lilac and teal indicate negative and positive contrasts, respectively (182 and 413 items). The dashed grey line marks zero. The teal point and horizontal interval show the equal-item mean, 0.014265, and its two-sided 95% confidence interval, 0.011769–0.016761. Uncertainty uses CR1 standard errors clustered by 553 source sentences and a Student t reference distribution with 552 degrees of freedom. The interval describes uncertainty in the mean, not the spread of individual items. Positive Δᵢ indicates closer alignment of M continuations with A than with I. Source data are provided.

**Fig. 3 | Overall association between contextual preference and continuation alignment.** Each point represents one target item (n = 595), with unadjusted scores on both axes. The line is the frozen M0 ordinary least squares fit. Shading indicates its pointwise 95% confidence band for the fitted mean, calculated from the full CR1 covariance matrix clustered by 553 source sentences, with 552 degrees of freedom. It is not a prediction interval for individual items. The slope is 0.003249 (95% confidence interval, 0.002032–0.004467); R² = 0.0402. Preference is expressed as an apt-minus-inapt log-probability contrast in nats. Complete regression results appear in Table 2. Source data are provided.

**Fig. 4 | Unadjusted and conditional associations of the context increment.** **a**, Raw Cᵢ and Δᵢ scores for all 595 target items (Pearson r = −0.0010). The line and pointwise 95% confidence band are a descriptive unadjusted fit, distinct from the two formal RQ2 models in Table 2. **b**, Both Cᵢ and Δᵢ are residualized against an intercept and S_word. Their partial Pearson correlation is 0.146, as annotated above the panel. The resulting added-variable slope equals the frozen M1 conditional coefficient, 0.002611 (95% confidence interval, 0.001283–0.003939). Shading maps the M1 CR1 slope interval onto the residual predictor, holding the residualizing projections fixed and anchoring the line at the residual origin. It represents conditional-slope uncertainty, not a full prediction band for the original outcome. Both panels show the same items with matching numerical axis spans. Uncertainty is clustered by 553 source sentences with 552 degrees of freedom. The full M1 R² is 0.0462; this is not the residual plot R². Source data are provided.
